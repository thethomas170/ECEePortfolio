ECE Roadmap

Career Goals

In ten years I want to be an onsite project manager responsible for electrical energy systems on large-scale construction, or a senior engineer at a major firm in the power sector. This page is the plan for getting there, written so that I can hold myself to it and revise it honestly as things change.

Why the power sector

I chose this direction for three reasons, in order of how much weight I give them.

The work is physical and accountable. I want a career where there is a site, a crew, a schedule, and a system that either energizes correctly or does not. I learned in a restaurant kitchen that I do my best work when the standard is concrete and the result is visible the same day. Electrical construction and power delivery have that quality in a way that few engineering paths do.

The demand is structural, not cyclical. Electricity consumption is rising sharply — data centers in particular are adding load faster than the delivery infrastructure can absorb — while a large share of the equipment carrying that load is near the end of its service life and in short supply. I researched this directly for my ECE Deep Dive on the large power transformer shortage. The conclusion I came to is that the people who know how to design, build, and commission this infrastructure are going to be needed for my entire working life.

It rewards breadth. Project management in this field requires that you understand the engineering well enough to be taken seriously by the engineers and well enough to be taken seriously by the crews. That combination is the version of this career I actually want, rather than specializing so narrowly that I only ever see one subsystem.

Thread selection

Georgia Tech's ECE program is organized into threads, and my choices follow directly from the goal above.

Primary

Electric Energy Systems

Generation, transmission, distribution, and the machines in between. This is the core of what I want to be responsible for professionally, and the thread that maps most directly onto utility and electrical construction work.

Primary

Circuit Technology

The component-level understanding that keeps me credible as an engineer rather than only as a manager. Power electronics is where a lot of the real innovation in grid hardware is happening right now.

Under consideration

Electronic Devices

Broad applicability and strong overlap with power electronics. I am keeping it open as an alternative second thread.

Under consideration

Signal & Information Processing

Relevant to grid monitoring, protection, and smart-grid instrumentation. A useful complement if I move toward the controls side.

The steps from here to there

A goal ten years out is only useful if it decomposes into things I can act on this semester. Here is the sequence I am working.

  1. Now — First year

    Build the foundation and the habit of making things

    Finish the math and computing sequence that everything else depends on, complete the ECE 1100 Discovery Project as a real hardware build rather than a writeup, and publish this ePortfolio so that there is a public record of my work from the start rather than assembled in a panic during junior year.

  2. Spring 2027

    Apply to a VIP team in power delivery

    Georgia Tech's Vertically Integrated Projects program is the most direct route I have into real research on power delivery and the power sector as an underclassman. This is my highest-priority application because it converts coursework into something I can actually point at.

  3. Summer 2027

    Internship in electrical construction project management

    I want site experience early. The target is an internship with an electrical contractor working on large-scale projects — the kind of work that puts electrical systems into stadiums, data centers, and industrial facilities. I am applying broadly for this cycle and treating every application and conversation as preparation for the next one, because this is the experience that would tell me fastest whether the management track is the right version of this career for me.

  4. Summer 2027 (alternate track)

    Georgia Tech China Summer Program

    I have applied to study in Xi'an, Qingdao, and Shanghai, taking Differential Equations and Physics II. The academic value is real, but the reason I want it is that nearly all of my experience abroad has been family travel to Vietnam, and I want to operate somewhere unfamiliar on my own terms.

  5. Years 2–4

    Depth, licensure track, and network

    Complete the Electric Energy Systems and Circuit Technology thread courses, take on a leadership role in a technical organization, pursue a co-op or a second internship with a utility or a large contractor, and sit for the FE exam so that the PE licensure path is open to me after graduation. Keep showing up to the ECE career fair early rather than only when I need something.

  6. Years 5–10

    From field engineer to running the job

    Start as a field or design engineer at a firm doing substantial electrical work, earn the PE, and move deliberately toward owning a scope: first as an assistant or project engineer, then as the onsite project manager accountable for the electrical systems on a major build. The alternative branch, if I find I prefer the technical depth to the management track, is senior engineer at a large firm in the same sector. Both are good outcomes. I do not need to choose yet, but I do need the experience that keeps both open.

Gaps I know I have

An honest roadmap names what is missing. Mine, right now:

  • Hands-on power hardware. My practical experience so far is low-voltage hobby electronics. I need coursework and site exposure to real power equipment.
  • Industry software. I have not yet worked in the design and analysis tools used in this field, and I intend to pick those up through VIP work and internships.
  • Formal project management training. If I am serious about the management track, I should be deliberate about learning scheduling, estimating, and contract basics rather than assuming they come automatically with seniority.
  • Professional network depth. I have started this through the ECE career fair and by maintaining contact with engineers I have met, but it is early and it needs consistent work rather than bursts of effort before deadlines.

Listing these is the point of the exercise. Each one is a reason to apply for something specific this year instead of next.
